Tree World

1. Mangrove tree

A mangrove is a specialized, salt-tolerant tree or shrub that grows in intertidal coastal zones across tropical and subtropical regions. Operating as nature's coastal buffer, these trees thrive in harsh environments where regular plants cannot survive—namely harsh saltwater, shifting tides, and oxygen-poor mud.

2.Fruit trees

A fruit tree is a woody perennial plant that bears edible, seed-containing fruits derived from its flowers. While botanically all flowering trees produce fruit, the term specifically refers to cultivated or wild trees producing fleshy fruits or culinary nuts consumed by humans and animals.

3.Flower tree

A flowering tree (or ornamental tree) is a woody perennial grown primarily for its prominent, decorative blossoms. While botanically all angiosperm trees produce flowers to reproduce, the term popularizes species that produce dramatic, colorful seasonal blooms—often filling entire canopies before or alongside leaf emergence.

4.Coal tree

Coal tree" refers to the ancient, giant lycophyte plants—most notably Lepidodendron (scale trees) and Sigillaria—that dominated vast tropical swamp forests during the Carboniferous Period (around 300 to 360 million years ago). Rather than living trees today, they are prehistoric plants whose buried, pressurized remains formed the majority of the world's coal deposits.
